---
title: When Books Come to Life
summary: On binge-reading Pachinko from a sickbed, seeing Min Jin Lee in San Francisco, and why great stories still matter as technology evolves.
---

After watching Min Jin Lee's 2026 Class Day speech at Yale on YouTube, I was hooked by her transparency, brilliance, and charisma. I immediately bought *Pachinko*, and then, just as immediately, didn't read it.

When I became so ill that I had to stay in bed for a few days, I binge-read *Pachinko* and fell hopelessly in love with its characters. It was so vividly written that I felt I could touch the moments of the protagonist's life as they unfolded.

As a big fan, I couldn't miss the chance to see Min Jin Lee when she came to San Francisco. I picked up an autographed copy of her new book, and now I'm waiting to get sick again so I can devote my whole life to it. (Isn't that what reading is?)

What struck me was how little she wanted to talk about herself, even though a packed audience had come to see her and only her. She was modest, even shy, and yet that same brilliance and depth shone through. Because we were in San Francisco, she said we were all changing the world, but she also seemed skeptical, even apprehensive. She reflected on AI and said it couldn't be compared to human experience, because it cannot sacrifice.

I'm so happy I saw her, and I'm more enthusiastic than ever about reading books, no matter how technology evolves. Great stories like *Pachinko* aren't just stories. They are art, snapshots of time, and portraits from brilliant minds.
